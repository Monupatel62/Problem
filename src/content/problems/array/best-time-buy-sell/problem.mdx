You are given an array `prices` where `prices[i]` is the stock price on day `i`. Choose at most one day to buy one share and one **later** day to sell it. Return the largest profit from that single transaction. If every possible sale loses money, do not trade and return 0.

The order is essential. In `[2, 4, 1]`, buying at 2 and selling at 4 earns 2. You cannot use the later price 1 as the buy price for the earlier sale at 4. In a decreasing series such as `[7, 6, 4, 3, 1]`, no valid future sale is more expensive than its earlier buy, so the answer is 0.

A direct method compares every buy day with every later sell day. It is easy to prove correct, but it can inspect roughly n² pairs. For up to one hundred thousand days, use a single left-to-right scan.

While reading prices, maintain the lowest price seen on any earlier day. Treat that as the best buy opportunity available before today. If today's price is `p`, then selling today after that best earlier buy would produce `p - minPrice`. Compare this candidate with the best profit found so far. After evaluating the candidate, update the minimum price when today is lower.

The result starts at zero because skipping the transaction is allowed. This strategy automatically respects buy-before-sell order, works with one-day input, repeated prices, rising prices, falling prices, and never modifies the input array. It returns only the profit, not the buy and sell indices.
